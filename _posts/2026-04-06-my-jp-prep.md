# Questions and Answers:

## 1. How did you shortlist universities and supervisors for your PhD applications?

Answer: In general, in AI era the best way to shortlist University is to use AI. You can use [Gemin](https://gemini.google.com/app) or [Chatgpt](https://chatgpt.com) or [Notebooklm](https://notebooklm.google). Best is Notebooklm in my opinion. You can add your CV and research plan as pdf or doc in notebooklm or Gemini or Chatgpt and then ask for which country will be best for you to do research on this topic. The much context you give AI the better they will find the Universities for you. Make sure to use deep research mode in any AI you are using (if the mode is available. That mode is available for the 3 AI's I told you about). Upon shortlisting Universities you ask chagpt to give you name of professors working on your research interest in that specific University. Then make sure you visit each professors profile it shortlisted and verify by yourself.

In my case, I wanted to go to Japan so I searched using chatgpt for University of Japan. It shortlisted me the best universities. Then I went to those University and verify by myself. For example,  in Nagoya University chatgpt suggested that many professors actively working on my research topic Multiple Zeta Values and Multiple Eisenstein series. So, I check ✅ by myself each professor it shortlisted in Nagoya University. I got two professors there. Now, I check their research interest and research papers names listed in their website and also check whether they were open to take PhD students or what kind of PhD students they wanted to take. Although these all you can ask AI to search for you. AI usually gives references from which they are claiming the facts. You make sure double check by visiting those references. Using AI is completely upto you to save your time. Otherwise you can just search manually. But the process is same. AI is just a tool. But use it carefully.


## 2. Which parts of your application do you think played the most important role in your selection?

Answer: After finding the professor whose research work is aligned with your research interest (Use again AI to shortlist specific professors research paper that closely matches your research interest), you can read the research paper's few pages. You also can use Notebooklm or Gemini to summerize the keykindings of the paper and which part to read for approaching the professors. This is the main part. Here you have to invest your time. Manually read the important sections that AI shortlist for you based on your research interest. Make sure to use Notebooklm or Gemini in this case. They are better for maths and research topics. 

In my specific case,
I didn't read research papers of my supervisor Henrik Bachmann initially. Instead I was lucky to get his [classnotes](https://www.henrikbachmann.com/uploads/7/7/6/3/77634444/mzv_2025_lecture_notes_v25.pdf)  on the research topic of my interest, which was compact and key researches and findings were combined there neatly with references of main research papers. I read the first chapter and then solved the exercises of the first chapter and started writing them neatly in a [PDF (latex)](https://doi.org/10.13140/RG.2.2.19279.55206). This is a good practice. You can do it when you read research papers. Write your key findings or what topic triggers your interest the most in the pdf and make a journal like pdf ready before you contact that professor. 
I solved some of the excercises in a unique way that attracts my professor attention. I also read one reference research paper cited in the lecture note and while reading that research paper I came accross one result that the paper didn't prove, just stated. I took help of Gemini and read the results required for writing a proof of  that result, then formulate a proof by myself and also included that in my journal (3.1.2 Main result in PDF). So, I can say this played a huge role in my selection.


## 3. Did you contact professors directly before applying? If yes, how did you approach them and what kind of responses did you usually receive?

Answer: I contacted my supervisor [Henrik Bachmann](https://www.henrikbachmann.com/) and told him my research interest along with my CV and I told him I am reading his lecture notes. In his case, he told he is mainly willing to take students who have great interest in this topic even though they may not have any background of this topic. I was among these category. I emailed him multiple times. He didn't respond in the beginning. Then finally oneday he responded. Make sure not to flood one professor multiple email. Just keep safe cooldown period of 1-2 week before sending follow up. Then he asked for sending some of my work on this topic to check my mathematical skill. I sent him the journal I made. He was then impressed a lot by my unique way of solving one question of the exercise (1.9.1 Question in [https://drive.google.com/file/d/1e9j8bRBBH3vW-wnJTEXKKJ4kl2slktTC/view?usp=drivesdk](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1e9j8bRBBH3vW-wnJTEXKKJ4kl2slktTC/view?usp=drivesdk)). He also mentioned he didn't even think before considering to solve that problem in such a way. For example I considered generating series as formal power series and then take derivative of it within its radius of convergence to prove a result. He mentioned he never thought before considering generating series as formal power series to take their derivative.

After this he agreed upon helping me for my application and recommendations in Nagoya G30 Programme. He guided me how to apply and help me to make my research plan that is the crucial thing to get selected for interview in G30 program in Nagoya. 

In other countries, the process is basically same. You have to contact a professor. If he agreed to take you as a PhD student then he will guide you the rest — how to apply and all. 

I emailed other professors while waiting for his response to the email he asked some of my works. Meanwhile I got positive and negative response both. One professor told me he is interested in me but can't take students for PhD as he is still junior. One of them told me he is not actively researching and taking students. So, postive in the way that they had interest in my CV and the journal but the situation was unfortunate. Some professors didn't replied. So, don't get demotivated. Keep the spirit high. This must take time. You have to be patient.

Note: You can directly send your journal to the professor if that is ready while you are contacting them. You can use same journal for different professors if the topic you are looking for matches exactly with the multiple professors you are contacting. Otherwise if you think you try different topics specific to different professors then make journal accordingly and send them. Make sure you start writing your journal before emailing professors. Because if they reply you and ask for your works, you will be in hurry and couldn't make the best of your work. Although if your MSc and PhD topic be same, you even can send your MSc thesis as your work. In my case I changed the topic completely (from topology to Number theory) that's why I have to focus on making the journal. In your case if you have a strong MSc thesis you don't have to worry in writing journal. But you always can make journal based on the research paper you are reading during application, this will be always an upper hand.


## 4. Are there any specific scholarships, fellowships, or fully funded programs that you would recommend I should seriously look into?

Answer: In Japan if you say, the main scholarship is [MEXT](https://www.in.emb-japan.go.jp/education/Research_Student.html). Otherwise there is [JSPS](https://www.jsps.go.jp/english/e-pd/). These are primary scholarships for foreign students. You can check them out if you are interested in Japan. 

In general, I don't have much idea about other countries. But these you will eventually know after you contact your professor. You can always use AI to find some best scholarship for a specific country.

## 5. Looking back now, what advice would you give to someone from our background who is planning to apply for a PhD abroad?

Answer: I think I have added the advices above. The journal making tip I got from AI honestly. So, main credit goes to Chagpt, Gemini and Notebooklm. But never be fully dependent on AI. They can make mistakes. Manually read research paper, only use AI to find the topics in the research paper that is of your use to save time. Manually verify everything that you got from AI. The best advice is to use AI to save time and know new things and find best research papers on any specific topic. But never blindly believe AI. Manually reverify everything it gave. Don't loose hope. Be patient. Don't flood professors inbox. They remain quite busy. Have faith in yourself. If your MSc thesis is strong then you have an upper hand. But still make sure to prepare a journal of what you find new and interesting in the research papers that you read. Don't just put whatever is exactly as it is in research papers. But write your opinion and new way of looking into it if possible and cite the research paper from which you took the problem and ideas in your journal.
